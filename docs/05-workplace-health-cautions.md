# Workplace and health-data cautions

**Dated October 27, 2026**

Extra care is needed when AI touches your job or anyone's health. This section is for workers, nurses, small business owners, and anyone handling information that belongs to other people.

> This is general education, not legal or medical advice. Your workplace policies and applicable laws (like HIPAA in the U.S.) override anything in this guide. When in doubt, ask your compliance, IT, or legal team.

## Workplace cautions

### 1. Your employer may see your AI chats

If you use an AI app through a work account (ChatGPT Enterprise, Claude Team, Microsoft 365 Copilot, etc.), your organization's admin can typically see usage reports and, in many cases, chat history. Don't assume a work AI account is private from your boss or IT team.

### 2. Don't paste company secrets

Avoid putting into any AI:

- Source code or internal documentation
- Customer or client lists and contact details
- Pricing, contracts, or unpublished financials
- Product plans or unreleased features
- Anything covered by an NDA

Once it's in the AI, you no longer control who might see it, even if you've opted out of training.

### 3. Use work-approved tools only

If your company provides an approved AI tool, use that instead of a personal account for work tasks. Approved tools usually come with contracts that limit how the provider uses your data.

### 4. Know your employer's AI policy

Many workplaces now have rules about what you can paste into AI, which apps are allowed, and whether AI can be used at all. Read it before you start.

## Health-data cautions (for healthcare workers and anyone handling health info)

### Personal health information is not safe in personal AI accounts

Do not paste into a personal AI chat:

- Patient names, medical record numbers, or any identifier
- Diagnoses, test results, medications, or treatment plans
- Photos of patients or clinical notes
- Anything that could identify a real person's health condition

### AI apps are not HIPAA-compliant by default

Standard consumer AI accounts (free ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.) are **not** HIPAA-compliant. Using them with patient data can violate privacy laws and your employer's policies, unless your organization has a specific agreement with the AI provider.

### What to do instead

- Ask about general health topics without naming anyone: "What are common signs of dehydration in older adults?" instead of naming a patient.
- Use de-identified, hypothetical examples only — and make sure they can't be traced back to a real person.
- If your workplace provides an approved, HIPAA-compliant AI tool, use it and follow your organization's rules.
- Never enter patient data into a "free" AI tool "just to see what it says."

### If you accidentally share patient data

1. Delete the chat immediately.
2. Report it to your supervisor, compliance officer, or privacy officer right away — don't wait.
3. Follow your organization's incident reporting process.
4. Prompt reporting can reduce harm and is usually viewed much more favorably than hiding a mistake.

## For small business owners and authors

If you run a small business (KDP publishing, eBay reselling, bookkeeping, etc.):

- Keep customer names, addresses, and order details out of AI chats.
- Don't paste tax records or bookkeeping data into personal AI accounts.
- If you use AI to help write listings or marketing, write from public information or your own words, not customer data.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] I know whether my employer can see my AI chat history
- [ ] I never paste passwords, API keys, or secrets into AI
- [ ] I never paste patient or client identifying information into AI
- [ ] I use work-approved AI tools for work tasks
- [ ] I've read my workplace's AI policy
- [ ] I know how to report an accidental data share at work
